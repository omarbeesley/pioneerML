# PURITY-model pedagogical figures

Figures for the PURITY model section (`sec:purity-model`): each conveys the
physical intuition behind one of the section's design choices or loss
functions. Each is generated by a standalone script importing the shared style
module `figstyle.py` (copied from `../ml_pedagogy`), and saved as vector `.pdf`
(for `\includegraphics`) and `.png` (for review). All quantities are
illustrative unless noted; the bias-width curves use the initialization values
of the learned parameters. Plots carry identifying labels only; the
explanations live in the captions below.

Render (all five):
```bash
source /home/obeesley/miniconda3/etc/profile.d/conda.sh && conda activate research
cd figures/purity_pedagogy
python fig_pinball_attenuated.py    # pinball hinge + sigma*=rho width calibration
python fig_chain_constraints.py     # composition vs exclusivity penalty surfaces
python fig_object_condensation.py   # physical vs clustering space, potential terms
python fig_attention_biases.py      # temporal coincidence gate, directional cone
python fig_robust_losses.py         # Huber bounded gradient, cosine loss
```

## LaTeX inclusion

Endpoint regression subsection (`fig_pinball_attenuated`):
```latex
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{figures/purity_pedagogy/fig_pinball_attenuated.pdf}
    \caption{The quantile-regression loss of the endpoint head. (a) The pinball loss for the three quantile levels. The hinge is asymmetric: at $q=0.84$ an under-prediction costs $0.84$ per unit of residual and an over-prediction costs $0.16$, so the minimizer sits at the $84\%$ quantile of the targets, and the three outputs together bracket the central $68\%$. (b) The scale-attenuated loss against the interval width $\sigma$ for three fixed pinball errors $\rho$, with the minima marked. The width is not a separate output: $\sigma$ is the gap between the predicted quantiles, so this term acts on the interval itself. The $\rho/\sigma$ wall punishes an interval narrower than the actual errors and the $\log\sigma$ term punishes indiscriminate width, so the minimum sits at $\sigma^{*}=\rho$ and a calibrated interval is the optimum of the loss. The practical payoff is for slices measured in only one strip view: the unmeasured transverse coordinate is reported with a wide asymmetric band, and the chain matching consumes these widths, so a poorly measured coordinate loosens its own compatibility window rather than faking precision.}
    \label{fig:pinball-attenuated}
\end{figure}
```

Chain matching subsection (`fig_chain_constraints`):
```latex
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{figures/purity_pedagogy/fig_chain_constraints.pdf}
    \caption{The chain-matching loss of Equation~\ref{eq:chain_loss} over the positron probabilities assigned to two candidate slices, for an event whose true positron is slice 1, with the trained coefficients $\lambda_\mathrm{comp}=0.2$ and $\lambda_\mathrm{excl}=10$ and one shared color scale (probabilities clipped at $10^{-3}$ to draw the finite surface). (a) The cross-entropy walls off the do-nothing strategy: it diverges as the true slice's probability $p_1$ goes to zero, and it charges probability placed on the unrelated slice. (b) The two constraint penalties combined. The surface is dominated by the exclusivity term, which equals the positron probability on the lesser slice and vanishes only when all of the positron probability sits on one slice; the composition term contributes a few percent of the trained scale in the upper-right corner. (c) The total. The star marks the minimum, at the decisive correct assignment $(1, 0)$. Point A is the indecision case $(0.55, 0.50)$, where both slices pass a $0.5$ readout threshold and the reconstructed positron time would blend two particles: the exclusivity term makes it expensive. Point B is the over-commitment case $(0.95, 0.40)$. The square at the origin, predicting no positron anywhere, is excluded by the cross-entropy wall.}
    \label{fig:chain-constraints}
\end{figure}
```

Object condensation subsection (`fig_object_condensation`):
```latex
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/purity_pedagogy/fig_object_condensation.pdf}
    \caption{Object condensation (illustrative). (a) Calorimeter hits in physical space: shower A has thrown an albedo fragment far from its core, and radioactivity hits are scattered across the detector, so a clustering by spatial proximity would split A and could absorb the radioactivity. (b) The same hits in the learned clustering space, where the loss gathers hits by origin: the fragment sits in shower A's group, the two condensation points (stars) are pushed at least the margin $m_c$ apart, and radioactivity is expelled beyond the blast margin $m_r$ from every seed (dotted circles). (c) The potential terms against clustering-space distance. The attraction is drawn for a committed seed-like hit and for a hit at the charge floor $q_{\min}$, which keeps ordinary members bound after their $\beta$ is driven down. The two repulsions are hinges that vanish beyond their margins, so a resolved pair stops generating gradient; the blast term reaches farther and pushes harder than the seed-seed term, holding radioactivity farther from a seed than two genuine showers are held from each other.}
    \label{fig:object-condensation}
\end{figure}
```

```latex
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/purity_pedagogy/fig_attention_biases.pdf}
    \caption{The two physics biases steering the event-builder attention, drawn with the initialization values of the learned widths. (a) The temporal attention factor against the track-to-cluster time difference. The window peaks at the expected time of flight $\tau_\mathrm{TOF}$ rather than at zero, since the cluster is expected after the track, and it widens for a low-energy cluster whose photostatistics-limited timing is poorer. (b) The directional bias against the alignment between the pion-stop-to-cluster direction and the reconstructed positron direction. A higher-energy cluster scatters less and points more sharply, so its width shrinks and its preference for aligned clusters steepens.}

\end{figure}
```

Pion stop / positron direction subsection (`fig_robust_losses`):
```latex
\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{figures/purity_pedagogy/fig_robust_losses.pdf}
    \caption{The two regression losses of the kinematic heads. (a) The Huber loss against the squared error. The two agree in the quadratic core $|\Delta|<\delta$, where the shrinking gradient lets the head settle precisely on the truth; beyond $\delta$ the Huber loss grows linearly and its gradient (dashed) saturates at $\pm\delta$, so a badly mismeasured event pulls on the weights with a bounded force and cannot dominate a batch update. (b) The cosine loss for the positron direction. Near alignment it follows its quadratic small-angle limit (dashed), and it saturates at $2$ for an anti-aligned prediction.}
    \label{fig:robust-losses}
\end{figure}
```
